Practical rule

Kelly is an upper-bound growth model. A fractional Kelly setting and conservative account-risk cap are generally more practical than using full Kelly.

FORMULA

What the calculator is doing

For win probability p, loss probability q = 1 − p, and payoff ratio b = average win ÷ average loss:

Full Kelly = (b × p − q) ÷ b

The result is the theoretical fraction of account equity to risk when a normal losing trade loses one unit of risk.

INTERPRETATION

A 15% winner does not mean risking 15%

If your average stock winner is 15% and your average loser is 10%, the payoff ratio is 1.5 to 1. If the app uses 2% account risk, an average win increases the account by about 3%, while an average loss reduces it by 2%.

Win impact = account risk × payoff ratio

TIME ESTIMATE

How the goal timeline is calculated

Each simulated trade is randomly recorded as a win or loss using your win rate. Equity compounds after every result. The app records the first trade on which each path reaches the target, then converts those trades into calendar time using your trades per week.

The most likely time is the median of successful simulated paths. Faster and slower estimates show the 25th and 75th percentiles.

AUTOMATIC SETTINGS

Why seed and maximum trades were removed

A random seed is only a technical tool used to reproduce the exact same random sequence. It does not describe your strategy, so the app now creates it automatically from your inputs.

A simulation still needs a safety horizon, but the app now calculates one automatically and shows it in the results instead of asking you to choose an arbitrary maximum-trades number.

LIMITATIONS

What Kelly does not know

Real trades are not perfectly independent. Slippage, gaps, changing volatility, strategy decay, correlated positions, taxes, fees, and estimation error can all make realized results worse than the model.

Use historical data across enough trades, test different market regimes, and treat the output as a range rather than a promise.

KellyLab is an educational modeling tool, not financial advice. Estimates are hypothetical and depend entirely on the assumptions entered.
